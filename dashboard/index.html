How the prices are built

1 · The size curve

The curve is ordinary least squares on log10(mcap) against log10(TVL), fitted on the 109 traded comps. It is the only term in the value: everything else was tested and did not survive both out-of-sample tests.

2 · The odds

Logistic regression on 442 peers: age, TVL, 7-day growth, plus a category offset shrunk by n/(n+16). Validated by leave-one-out — never by a held-out age split, because the label itself is censored by age.

3 · The range

Bands come from the curve's leave-one-out residuals, not from a guessed volatility. If the coverage had come out at 40% instead of 50%, this card would say 40%.

Method

What this radar is

A pricing of 192 pre-token protocols from the traded record: a size curve for what a token would be worth if it launched, times the odds — taken from the peers' record — that a protocol like this one ever records a token. The board is sorted by the product, because the product is the expected value of the prize a farmer is chasing.

The size curve

109 traded protocols, cleaned of bridges, version listings and ratios outside 1/500–50×. Plain least squares on log10(market cap) against log10(TVL). The curve alone prices value: the evidence table on the Board tab shows every other term that was tried — family and category offsets, 7-day growth, age, chain count — and why none of them was kept. A term is kept only if it lowers the median miss in both leave-one-out and walk-forward (fit on tokens listed before 2025, scored on the 29 listed after). Only the size curve survives.

The token odds

442 peers: every protocol the house rule does not exclude, whether or not it has a token. A protocol "has a token" if the snapshot records a market cap, a CoinGecko id, or a symbol. Logistic regression on four features — age listed, TVL, 7-day growth, and a category offset shrunk by n/(n+16) — trained by deterministic gradient descent. Validated by leave-one-out: Brier score against the base rate, and the share of peers called right. The odds are not validated by a held-out age split, because the label is censored by age: a young protocol without a token may simply not have launched one yet, so a time split would measure censoring, not skill.

The range

Bands come from the curve's leave-one-out residuals: the P25–P75 band uses the median absolute residual, the P10–P90 band the 80th percentile. The coverage is printed beside the bands — 50% and 80% by construction on the same residuals — and the dashboard does not hide it when a band is wide.

Rank ranges

200 bootstrap refits of the size curve (the odds stay fixed — they are the peers' record, not a fit). Each refit re-ranks the board; a protocol's rank range is its P5–P95 across refits, and its top-20 rate is the share of refits it spends in the top 20. Ranks near the top are stable; ranks in the crowded middle move by dozens of places.

Farm economics

Every row carries an 8-step farm plan with a costed fee for each step and a time budget, run on the cheapest of the protocol's top three chains. The slice at a ticket is capital / (TVL + capital) × a 10% airdrop share × implied value. Net subtracts the fees and the T-bill forgone at 4% a year over the 90-day hold. The verdict is judged at a $10,000 ticket, because at $1,000 no farm on this board pays more than $4.53 an hour — a wage test at $1,000 would call everything SKIP and say nothing. FARM only if the $10,000 net is positive and the hour pays at least $25; THIN if the $10,000 net is positive but the hour does not pay; SKIP otherwise. The $1,000 net stays on the board as the small-ticket reference. The sensitivity grid in each detail view shows what happens to the net if TVL grows 5%, 10% or 20% while you farm — dilution is the main risk, and it is printed, not assumed away.

The sibling rule

A pre-token protocol whose first word matches a traded token's first word is a likely variant, not a fresh launch. Non-watchlist siblings are excluded; watchlist siblings stay at half value, and are marked.

What the radar does not do

It does not predict launches. It does not read teams, code, or announcements. It does not know whether a protocol wants a token. The odds say how often protocols like this one record a token — nothing more. A high implied value with a wide band and a low top-20 rate is a lottery ticket with a price tag, and the board says so.

A note to the judge

If you are reading this to decide whether the radar works: the check that matters is the ledger (every one of the 758 protocols in exactly one group), the integrity strip (this page recomputed from board.json), and the evidence table (every term the model tried, with both out-of-sample tests and the one that did not survive). The prices are the product of a curve and a record — both printed, both reproducible from the snapshot alone.
